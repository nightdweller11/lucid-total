# Map study brief — five explorations of the company world

> Lead's brief, 2026-10-28 evening (T30). A **dedicated study, built separately**: nothing in this folder touches `docs/04-mocks/index.html`, `gen/` or `shots/` (the v0.2 gallery being built in parallel), and nothing in the docs is changed by the study. Its output is this folder: `STUDY.md`, `index.html` (one page, five variants), `gen/`, `shots/`.

## The owner's words

"The direction is right overall. The map doesn't feel organic enough, and it doesn't feel like something you zoom into. Showing data layers is also something I expect: cost layers, progress layers, issues layers, dependency layers. We don't want a map for a map's sake; it should make visual sense and help the user organise and navigate things, serve as a right and useful way of understanding the company, its processes, its people, its tasks. A dedicated study with variants and variations, Jony Ive and Steve Jobs. I like the gamified concept, and other concepts can be explored: map elevation levels (a little 3D would look futuristic, with the relevant data floating on it), heat maps, overlays you see in maps (think SimCity: electricity, plumbing) that illustrate how things work and identify problems (a clogged pipe as the analogy). Five variations, as different from one another as possible, each a different exploration. And always: zooming in to see the next layer and dig deeper."

## What the map must do, whatever the metaphor

Read `docs/13-experience.md` (binding: attention budget §3, places §5, facades §5.3, honesty §5.4) and `docs/03-map-and-views.md` (zoom levels, perspective, layers, phone), plus `docs/research/interface-inspiration.md` §2 (places with identity, Pad++, CK3, Kumu showcase) and §4 (Tableau Pulse, Activity rings). Each variant must answer, at every depth, the five questions (what exists, what moves, what is stuck, what needs me, how it connects) and must:

1. **Zoom in to reveal.** Three depths drawn for every variant: the company; one domain (Engineering or Marketing); one program (Private beta · Wave 71 with its streams and its buildings, where the products are visible as facades). The child must be visible in the parent before zoom, and the transition must be imaginable as continuous (draw the three depths so that a viewer can see what becomes what).
2. **Data layers.** At least four layers drawn for every variant, each as its own still over the same company depth: **cost** (spend and AI cost by place), **progress** (done share and forecast), **issues** (support issues, incidents, risk), **dependencies** (roads/flows/pipes, the critical path, the blocked link), and a fifth of the variant's own (people and load, attention, trust, time). A layer changes how the terrain is drawn (tint, elevation, flow, glow), never just adds badges.
3. **One problem picture.** Every variant shows the same stuck situation: the Railway token chore blocks the dispatcher work, which blocks the store submission, and the Compliance DPA signature is late; Marketing waits on both. Draw it in the variant's own language (a clogged pipe, a dark district, a cut power line, a broken orbit, a delayed line) so that the problem is found without reading.
4. **Identity.** Places keep a name, a subject, a glyph, a hue and a landmark (reuse the landmark idea from the v0.2 brief; you may draw your own set in the variant's style).
5. **Attention budget** (`docs/13` §3) at every depth and layer: one focal subject, few labels, no counts on the terrain, alarms only for failing states, at most two disclosure levels. Breathing room. Nothing that looks like a dashboard grid or a kanban.
6. **Phone**: one still per variant of the company depth at 390 px to show the metaphor survives a phone (one place at a time is allowed).
7. **Lucid Glass**: the three themes' tokens (copy from `../v0.1/gen/css.mjs` as values), Sora/Figtree, Lucide only for buttons; everything else SVG you draw. A little 3D is welcome (isometric or oblique projection, extruded relief, parallax strata) as long as it is still legible in high contrast and RTL (draw the Deep Blue theme for all; add Sky Glass for the company depth of each variant).

## The five variants (as different as possible)

| # | Name | Metaphor | What carries data | Zoom | Problem picture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **V1 Relief** | Elevation terrain, oblique 3D | The company as a landscape in relief: height = importance or remaining work (choose and state), light = progress, haze in the valleys = staleness; data layers float as translucent strata above the ground (a cost stratum with contour lines, an issues stratum as weather); roads along the valleys | Flying lower: the domain's valley fills the frame, its programs as terraces, the program's buildings on the terrace | A landslide blocking the valley road; the stuck chore as a boulder |
| **V2 City** | SimCity / gamified | Places as districts; buildings = items, height = effort, lit windows = progress, finished landmarks; **infrastructure layers**: power (decisions and goals feeding work; a cut line = a missing decision), plumbing (the flow of work through states as pipes with pressure; a clogged pipe = a stuck process, backed-up items visible), roads (dependencies and traffic), zoning (domains); day/night = activity; a tiny population = people where they work | Street level: the program as a block, its buildings with facades as shopfronts | A burst or clogged pipe at the dispatcher with items backed up; a dark district (Compliance) with the power line cut at the DPA |
| **V3 Biome** | Living organism, microscope | Places as organic cells that grow and divide (sub-places as nuclei inside, visible before zoom), vessels carrying flow between them (pulse = velocity), health as hue, scar tissue = lessons, fever = heat; layers as stains (a cost stain, a risk stain, a trust stain) | A microscope step: the cell fills the view, its organelles are the programs, the program's granules are the buildings | A blocked vessel swelling before the dispatcher; the DPA as a membrane that will not open |
| **V4 Orbits** | Star system, telescope | Goals as suns, programs as planets orbiting them (distance = time to delivery, orbit speed = velocity), items as moons and satellites, people as ships, dependencies as gravitational tethers, risks as asteroid fields; layers as spectral filters (cost in infrared, issues in X-ray, progress as luminosity) | The telescope: from the system to one planet to its surface, where the buildings are stations with facades | A moon caught in a decaying orbit (the dispatcher) with a tether pulling the store submission planet; the DPA as an eclipse over Compliance |
| **V5 Lines** | Transit network, flow | The company's processes as lines (idea → decision → work → release → feedback; lead → customer → issue → resolution), stations as milestones and gates, trains as items in motion, crowding = congestion, a delayed line = a slipped date, interchanges = overlaps between programs; places are the districts the lines pass through, drawn faintly behind; layers as service maps (cost per line, issues as disruptions, dependencies as connections) | Entering a station: the station becomes the program's district plan with its platforms (streams) and its buildings | A service disruption on the Store line between Dispatcher and Submission, with trains waiting; "replacement bus" = the manual workaround |

For each variant draw: the three depths (company, domain, program), four or five layer stills at company depth, the problem picture, the phone still, and one Sky Glass company still. That is about 10–11 stills per variant, 52–55 in all. Keep every still inside the attention budget and state the counts in its caption (needs-you · text above body size · numbers).

## The study document (`STUDY.md`, 2,500–4,000 words)

1. The questions the map must answer and the criteria: organic feel, zoomability (does the child live in the parent?), data layers (do they change the terrain?), navigation clarity, finding problems without reading, scale growth (two people to two hundred), phone, accessibility and RTL, attention budget, rendering cost (SVG, Canvas, WebGL), implementation risk.
2. One section per variant: the idea, what it is best at, where it breaks, how it handles overlaps (a program across domains), perspectives (CMO vs CTO), the Refocus simulation (how effort moving would look), the phone, and the two or three things worth taking even if the variant is not chosen.
3. A comparison table with honest scores.
4. A recommendation: one variant, or a hybrid with the exact elements taken from the others, and what it means for `docs/03` and `packages/scene`. The lead decides; write the case.
